## How the team was formed?

The team EatChiwi was formed in an unexpected way, starting with how the members came together:

- Efraín J. Ortiz Mulero was already participating in the Future Engineer category and had a solid amount of experience in both mechanics and programming. He is also the brother of José F. Ortiz Mulero, who later joined the Future Engineer category.

- José had previously participated in RoboMission, where he served as both the mechanic and programmer for his team. He was very dedicated to his work and constantly sought growth, which led him to join Future Engineer. Being teamed up with someone he already knew and lived with made this an even better opportunity. 

- Faneshka Cartagena Maldonado was participating in RoboSports, where she worked as an engineer, programmer, and in charge of documentation for her team. At one point, one of her teammates left their all girls group, leaving her with just one remaining teammate, who eventually left as well. This situation led her to explore new opportunities, and she decided to join the Future Engineer category. Since the team only needed one more member to be complete, she became the perfect addition.

 The three of them already knew each other well, which helped everything come together naturally. As for the name “EatChiwi”, it comes from Faneshka’s guinea pig, named Chiwi. Efraín thought it would be funny to name the team EatChiwi, since they often joked about how guinea pigs are eaten in some cultures.
 
 The team roles were assigned based on each member’s strengths: Efraín took on the role of mechanic, as he excelled with the 3D printer and designs, Faneshka became responsible for documentation because of her strong note taking skills, and José focused on programming, as he worked best with coding and learned quickly.

I participated in RoboSports for a brief period in 2024 as a substitute. Later that same year, I joined Future Engineers and participated in the 2024 Open Championship in Puerto Rico, where we placed 4th.

During 2024, I was the mechanical and electrical engineer for the vehicle. I was responsible for different aspects of the vehicle’s construction, design, and electrical systems.

Our current team was formed after a selection of the best members of the club, which was decided by the coach and me. The previous team was not performing as expected, so we decided to make a change and form a new team for Future Engineers.

Currently, I continue to be the mechanical and electrical engineer for the vehicle. I mainly work on the mechanical and electrical aspects of the autonomous car, including its construction, design, and overall functionality. Right now, we are also preparing to represent Puerto Rico on December 7 at the upcoming competition.

### José Fabian Ortiz Mulero:

 My name is José F. Ortiz Mulero, and I joined the robotics club during 10th grade. I decided to join because I had already developed an interest in robotics, and since my brother was already part of the club, I saw it as an opportunity to expand my knowledge and develop new skills.

The category I joined was Robot Mission Senior. Since it was the only category with an open spot, I decided to take the opportunity. I ended up becoming the mechanic on my team, but I also participated and learned about the other areas of the team. Through this experience, I gained valuable knowledge about robotics, teamwork, problem-solving, and responsibility.

My team worked hard and eventually competed at the national level, where we placed 2nd. This achievement gave us the opportunity to travel to Singapore and represent Puerto Rico at the international competition. Being able to compete internationally was an incredible experience and motivated me to continue growing in robotics.

After that, another opportunity came along that allowed me to continue developing my skills and pursue my interest in becoming a future engineer. I joined the same team as my brother and one of my friends, but this time I took on a new role as the programmer. This has allowed me to learn even more about robotics, programming, and how the different parts of a robot work together.

Now, we are looking forward to another important opportunity: representing Puerto Rico in this category this December. I am excited to continue learning, improving my skills, and giving my best alongside my teammates.

### Faneshka Cartagena Maldonado:

My name is Faneshka Cartagena Maldonado. I joined the robotics club during 10th grade. I joined the robotics club because one of my mutual friends was interested in the club, and I wanted to understand why it was so special to them. Soon, I found out why. This whole process was new to me since I didn’t really have an interest in robotics before joining.

I had an open spot in RoboSports, and I was placed on a team that had already competed in 2024 and had the opportunity to travel to Turkey. At first, my role was to document things and sketch the models. Later, the team went through some changes, and I became the mechanic while also being present and helping in the other areas of the team. The team also got a new name, Sirens.

With Sirens, we went to Nationals and placed first. We then had the opportunity to go to an international competition, where my team traveled and represented Puerto Rico. After that, my team eventually broke apart, leaving me looking for another opportunity to continue growing in robotics.

That is when I saw an opportunity with Future Engineers and decided to take it. I found two of my friends there as well, which made the experience even better. I also took on a new role. This time, I became the person responsible for documenting our work, and I was also the one who created our team logo.

We went on to win first place at Nationals, and now we are looking forward to representing Puerto Rico in this category. I am excited to continue learning, growing, and gaining more experience through robotics while representing Puerto Rico alongside my teammates.
